Story Starter: App Map

Where everything lives, and where to start when you want to change something.

What it is

A phone-friendly page that plans the start of a Sims 4 story. You pick how you like to start, describe your idea, answer a couple of follow-up questions, pick packs, and set mood dials. You get back a deck of plan cards, saved on your device.

The page

public/. Every screen, the collage look, and your saved plans (browser storage). It never talks to the AI directly.

The little server

server/. Serves the page, keeps the API key private, draws random story ingredients, and asks Claude for the plan.

The notes

data/. Pack notes, dial wording, starting styles, nudges, real Create-a-Sim options, and names. This is how the app "knows Sims."

Claude

Called by server/claude.js. Does the writing: the reflection, the follow-up questions, and the plan.

One action, end to end: "Make my plan"

On a phone, swipe the diagram sideways.

A route through the code (3 stops)

A reference route. Open each file and search for the anchor to land on the right spot.

1

The random draw: where "not generic" comes from

server/draw.js, search for export function drawIngredients(

Before Claude writes anything, the code picks 2 character types, 2 hooks, a twist, and a setting from the packs you chose. Packs take turns so each one contributes, the dials make matching ingredients likelier, and the supernatural switch filters them. A seed makes a draw repeatable for demos.

Why it matters

The randomness lives in code, not in the AI's habits. That's the core idea of the app: structure instead of "AI, give me ideas."

2

The instructions: how a plan gets its shape

server/prompt.js, search for export function buildPlanPrompt(, then How to build the plan and const DIAL_EFFECTS

Everything Claude is told: your idea and answers, the drawn ingredients (a menu, use only 1 or 2), what each dial position must do, the story frame (want, flaw, opposition, day one, clues), the real Create-a-Sim options your packs allow, and the names to use. parsePlan then checks the answer has every part.

Why it matters

Most "the plan feels off" problems are fixed here, in plain-English rules, not in code.

3

The deck: how the plan becomes cards

public/js/screens.js, search for export function planCards(

Turns the plan into tiles, orders them by your starting style (character-first opens on a Sim), and gives each a pattern, a tilt, and its turn in the deal. public/js/store.js (savePlan) keeps it on your device.

Why it matters

If you add a new field to the plan, this is where it shows up on a card.

To change this, start here

  • Add or fix a pack's story notes: data/pack-notes.json (same shape as the Vampires entry; packs without notes show "notes coming soon").
  • Reword a dial position: data/dials.json. To change what a dial makes Claude do: DIAL_EFFECTS in server/prompt.js.
  • Change the story rules: "How to build the plan" in server/prompt.js.
  • Starting styles and nudges: data/starting-styles.json, data/nudges.json.
  • Welcome copy: welcomeScreen in public/js/screens.js.
  • Colors, type, motion: public/css/styles.css (the color tokens are at the top). Patterns and shapes: edit tools/make_svgs.mjs, then run node tools/make_svgs.mjs.
  • The AI model or how hard it thinks: server/claude.js (MODEL and effort).

A practice to reuse

When an idea grows mid-build, check it against the kernel, then build the smallest version that serves it.

What happened: plans had vivid characters but flat, reactive stories. A full story frame was proposed (seven new card lines and an extra slice). Before agreeing, the question "am I over-engineering?" was checked against devpost/scope.md > The Unique Kernel ("structure instead of 'AI, give me ideas'").

The evidence: the fix did serve the kernel, so it was kept, but as a lean version: the frame lives in the instructions (server/prompt.js), and the cards show just "Wants", "Holding them back", and "Day one". The Revisions list in devpost/checklist.md records this alongside the other mid-build growth (Create-a-Sim details, names, the restyle), and the build still finished every slice and the final review.

On your next project: keep the one-line kernel somewhere visible. When a new idea comes up mid-build, ask two questions: does it serve the kernel, and what's the smallest version that does? Then write the decision down where the plan lives.

Run it again

From the project folder, with your key in .env:

npm start

Then open the address it prints (http://localhost:3000 on this computer, or the "On your phone" address on the same Wi-Fi). The README.md has phone troubleshooting.
